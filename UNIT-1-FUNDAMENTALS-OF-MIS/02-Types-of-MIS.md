# 🔥 Let's Begin With a Question

Imagine a large organization such as a bank, university, supermarket, hospital, or e-commerce company.

Thousands of activities happen every day.

For example:

- Customers purchase products.
- Employees record attendance.
- Students register for courses.
- Customers make payments.
- Managers monitor sales.
- Executives examine company performance.

Now imagine that the organization gives **exactly the same information to everyone**.

### 🤔 Would that work?

Obviously, **no**.

A cashier needs information about today's transactions.

A department manager needs information about department performance.

A senior executive needs information about the organization's overall performance.

A strategic manager may need information about future opportunities and threats.

Therefore:

> **Different people need different types of information for different purposes.**

This is why organizations use **different types of information systems**.

---

# 1. What Are Types of Information Systems?

An organization normally does not depend on a single information system.

Different information systems are designed to support different:

- Business activities
- Users
- Management levels
- Decisions
- Information requirements
- Organizational functions

Therefore, information systems can be classified according to the **purpose they serve and the users they support**.

A simple way to understand this is:

```text
BUSINESS ACTIVITIES
        ↓
       DATA
        ↓
INFORMATION SYSTEMS
        ↓
USEFUL INFORMATION
        ↓
    DECISIONS

Different systems perform different roles within this overall process.
