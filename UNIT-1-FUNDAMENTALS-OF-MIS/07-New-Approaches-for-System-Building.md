# 🔥 Let's Begin With a Question

Imagine that a university wants to develop a new ERP system.

The university needs the system to manage:

- Student admission
- Attendance
- Fees
- Examination
- Results
- Faculty information
- Courses
- Student communication

Now imagine that the university spends **two years** building the complete system.

After two years, the system is finally delivered.

But then students and faculty say:

> "This is not what we actually needed."

The system may technically work, but it may not solve the real problems of its users.

### 🤔 What went wrong?

The organization may have spent too much time building the system without continuously involving the people who would actually use it.

This is one of the reasons organizations have developed **new approaches to system building**.

The basic idea is:

    OLD APPROACH
          ↓
    PLAN EVERYTHING
          ↓
    BUILD EVERYTHING
          ↓
    TEST AT THE END
          ↓
    DELIVER SYSTEM

Modern approaches often follow:

    UNDERSTAND PROBLEM
          ↓
    BUILD SMALL PART
          ↓
    GET USER FEEDBACK
          ↓
    IMPROVE
          ↓
    TEST
          ↓
    RELEASE
          ↓
    CONTINUE IMPROVING

Therefore:

> **Modern system building focuses more on flexibility, user involvement, faster development, continuous improvement, and changing business requirements.**

# 1. What Is System Building?

Before understanding new approaches, we need to understand what system building means.

> **System building is the process of developing, acquiring, implementing, and improving an information system to solve an organizational problem or meet a business requirement.**

In simple language:

> **System building means creating or obtaining an information system that helps an organization perform its work better.**

For example:

A university may build or acquire a system for:

- Student management
- Attendance
- Examination
- Fees
- Library management

A bank may build systems for:

- Account management
- Online banking
- Loan processing
- Transactions

An e-commerce company may build systems for:

- Product management
- Orders
- Payments
- Inventory
- Delivery

---

# 2. Why Do Organizations Build Information Systems?

Organizations build information systems because they need to:

- Solve business problems
- Improve efficiency
- Reduce manual work
- Improve customer service
- Manage information
- Support decision-making
- Automate repetitive activities
- Connect departments
- Create new digital services
- Respond to changing markets

A simple relationship is:

    BUSINESS PROBLEM
          ↓
    SYSTEM REQUIREMENT
          ↓
    INFORMATION SYSTEM
          ↓
    BUSINESS SOLUTION
          ↓
    BETTER PERFORMANCE

# 3. Traditional Approach to System Building

Traditionally, organizations often followed a relatively structured sequence.

A simple version is:

    IDENTIFY PROBLEM
          ↓
    COLLECT REQUIREMENTS
          ↓
    DESIGN SYSTEM
          ↓
    DEVELOP SYSTEM
          ↓
    TEST SYSTEM
          ↓
    IMPLEMENT SYSTEM
          ↓
    MAINTAIN SYSTEM

This approach can be useful when requirements are stable and clearly understood.

However, modern business environments change rapidly.

---

# 4. Why Traditional Approaches May Become Difficult

Imagine an organization begins developing a system in January.

The requirements are decided in January.

But by September:

- Customer expectations change.
- Business processes change.
- Technology changes.
- Competitors introduce new services.
- Management changes priorities.
- Users discover new requirements.

If the development process is very rigid, changing the system may become difficult or expensive.

This creates a major challenge:

> **How can organizations build systems while remaining flexible enough to respond to change?**

This question led to several newer approaches.

    DIGITAL INFORMATION
          ↓

# 🧪 8. Prototyping

## 8.1 What Is Prototyping?

> **Prototyping is an approach in which an early version or model of a system is created so that users can examine it and provide feedback.**

In simple language:

> **Instead of building the complete system immediately, create a small working model first.**

For example, before building a complete university ERP, developers may create a simple prototype of:

- Student registration
- Attendance screen
- Faculty dashboard

Users can then see how the system may work.

---

# 9. Why Is Prototyping Useful?

Users often find it difficult to explain exactly what they need using only words.

For example:

A faculty member may say:

> "I want a better attendance system."

But what does "better" mean?

Maybe the faculty member wants:

- Faster entry
- Student search
- Automatic percentage calculation
- Monthly reports
- Mobile access

A prototype allows the user to see something concrete.

The process becomes:

    USER REQUIREMENT
          ↓
       PROTOTYPE
          ↓
      USER FEEDBACK
          ↓
      IMPROVEMENT
          ↓
       BETTER DESIGN

---

# 10. Example – University ERP Prototype

Suppose a university wants a new attendance system.

Instead of developing the entire system immediately:

### Step 1

Create a basic attendance screen.

### Step 2

Show it to faculty members.

### Step 3

Faculty members provide feedback.

For example:

> "We need a search option."

> "We need the previous attendance record."

> "We need automatic percentage calculation."

### Step 4

Developers modify the prototype.

### Step 5

Users test it again.

This process may continue until the design becomes more suitable.

---

# 11. Advantages of Prototyping

Prototyping can help organizations:

- Understand user requirements
- Identify problems early
- Obtain user feedback
- Reduce misunderstanding
- Improve system design
- Increase user acceptance

---

# 12. Limitations of Prototyping

Prototyping also has limitations.

For example:

- Users may think the prototype is the final system.
- Too many changes may increase development time.
- Developers may focus heavily on appearance rather than complete system quality.
- Poorly managed prototypes may create technical problems.

Therefore:

> **Prototyping is useful when it is properly planned and managed.**

---

# 13. 🎯 Classroom Question

Ask students:

> **Would you rather explain your ideal mobile application only through words, or see a basic version and then suggest changes?**

Most students will prefer seeing a basic version.

This is the basic idea behind prototyping.

# ⚡ 14. Agile Development

## 14.1 What Is Agile Development?

> **Agile development is an approach to system development that emphasizes flexibility, continuous user involvement, frequent delivery, teamwork, and response to changing requirements.**

In simple language:

> **Agile means developing a system in small parts, getting feedback frequently, and improving the system continuously.**

Instead of waiting until the entire system is completed:

    COMPLETE SYSTEM
          ↓
       WAIT
          ↓
       TEST
          ↓
      DELIVER

Agile often follows:

    SMALL PART
       ↓
     TEST
       ↓
    FEEDBACK
       ↓
   IMPROVEMENT
       ↓
   NEXT PART
       ↓
    REPEAT

---

# 15. Why Is Agile Important?

Modern businesses change quickly.

Suppose an e-commerce company is developing a shopping application.

During development, customers may request:

- Faster checkout
- New payment options
- Better search
- Product recommendations
- Order tracking

Agile development allows the organization to respond to changing requirements more easily.

---

    DEVELOP

    IMPROVE

# 17. Agile and User Involvement

One important feature of Agile is continuous communication with users or business representatives.

Users can help identify:

- What is useful
- What is unnecessary
- What needs improvement
- What should be developed next

Therefore:

> **Agile tries to keep the system development process closely connected with actual business needs.**

---

# 18. Agile and Scrum

One commonly used framework associated with Agile is **Scrum**.

Scrum organizes development work into short cycles called **sprints**.

A sprint is a limited period during which a team works on a selected set of tasks.

A simplified process is:

    REQUIREMENTS
          ↓
      SPRINT PLAN
          ↓
      DEVELOPMENT
          ↓
        TESTING
          ↓
       REVIEW
          ↓
       FEEDBACK
          ↓
    NEXT SPRINT

The exact practices used by organizations may vary.

---

# 19. Scrum Roles

For basic understanding, students should know three commonly discussed Scrum roles:

### Product Owner

Helps represent business needs and prioritize what should be developed.

### Scrum Master

Helps the team follow the Scrum process and removes obstacles.

### Development Team

Builds and tests the product.

A simple framework is:

    PRODUCT OWNER
          ↓
     WHAT TO BUILD
          ↓
    DEVELOPMENT TEAM
          ↓
      BUILD + TEST
          ↑
    SCRUM MASTER
    SUPPORTS PROCESS

---

# 20. Agile Meetings

Scrum commonly involves regular activities such as:

- Sprint planning
- Daily Scrum
- Sprint review
- Sprint retrospective

### Sprint Planning

The team decides what work to undertake during the sprint.

### Daily Scrum

A short team meeting to discuss progress and obstacles.

### Sprint Review

The team demonstrates completed work.

### Sprint Retrospective

The team reflects on what went well and what should improve.

For BBA students, remember:

> **Agile emphasizes short development cycles, regular feedback, teamwork, and continuous improvement.**

---

# 21. Advantages of Agile

Agile can provide:

- Faster delivery of useful features
- Better response to changing requirements
- Frequent user feedback
- Continuous improvement
- Better visibility of progress
- Greater collaboration

---

# 22. Limitations of Agile

Agile may become difficult when:

- Users are unavailable for feedback.
- Requirements are not prioritized.
- Teams lack coordination.
- Scope keeps changing without control.
- Organizations expect everything to be fixed from the beginning.

Therefore:

> **Agile requires communication, discipline, teamwork, and active participation.**

# 🔄 23. Iterative Development

## 23.1 What Is Iterative Development?

> **Iterative development means developing and improving a system through repeated cycles rather than trying to create the perfect system in one attempt.**

In simple language:

> **Build → test → learn → improve → repeat.**

The basic cycle is:

    PLAN
      ↓
    BUILD
      ↓
    TEST
      ↓
    FEEDBACK
      ↓
    IMPROVE
      ↓
    REPEAT

---

# 24. Example of Iterative Development

Suppose a company is developing an employee portal.

### Version 1

Basic login and profile.

### Version 2

Add leave management.

### Version 3

Add attendance.

### Version 4

Add payroll information.

### Version 5

Add reports.

The system improves through multiple versions.

---

# 25. Agile vs Iterative Development

These concepts are related but not exactly identical.

### Iterative Development

Focuses on:

> **Repeated improvement through multiple cycles.**

### Agile Development

Uses iterative and incremental development while emphasizing:

- Flexibility
- Collaboration
- User feedback
- Frequent delivery
- Response to change

A simple relationship is:

    ITERATIVE DEVELOPMENT
          +
    USER COLLABORATION
          +
    FLEXIBILITY
          +
    FREQUENT DELIVERY
          ↓
        AGILE

# 60. 🧠 Traditional vs Modern System Building

| Traditional Approach | Modern Approaches |
|---|---|
| More sequential | More iterative |
| Requirements often fixed early | Requirements can evolve |
| Feedback may occur later | Frequent feedback |
| Large delivery at the end | Frequent smaller releases |
| Less flexibility | Greater flexibility |
| Users may be involved less frequently | Users often participate continuously |
| Changes can be expensive later | Changes can be incorporated earlier |
| More emphasis on complete planning | More emphasis on learning and adaptation |

Important:

> **Traditional approaches are not always wrong.**

For stable and clearly defined requirements, structured approaches can still be useful.

The key idea is:

> **The choice of approach should depend on the nature of the project, organization, requirements, risks, and users.**

---

### Stable Requirements

# 70. ⚠️ Common Student Mistakes

### Mistake 1

Thinking Agile means:

> "No planning."

### Correct Understanding

Agile includes planning but allows plans and priorities to adapt as the project develops.

---

### Mistake 2

Thinking Prototyping means:

> "The prototype is always the final system."

### Correct Understanding

A prototype is generally an early model used for learning, feedback, and improvement.

---

### Mistake 3

Thinking Cloud means:

> "Data is stored somewhere on the Internet without any management."

### Correct Understanding

Cloud computing involves computing resources and services delivered through network-based infrastructure.

---

### Mistake 4

Thinking Outsourcing means:

> "The company has no IT department."

### Correct Understanding

An organization can have internal IT staff and still outsource selected activities.

---

### Mistake 5

Thinking Low-Code means:

> "No IT expertise is required."

### Correct Understanding

Low-code and no-code tools can simplify development, but professional IT expertise may still be required for complex systems, security, integration, governance, and architecture.

---

### Mistake 6

Thinking AI can completely replace developers.

### Correct Understanding

AI can assist development, but human review, testing, security, and business understanding remain important.
