# 1. What Is IT Infrastructure?

In simple words:

> **IT Infrastructure is the collection of technology resources that an organization uses to operate, store, process, communicate, and manage information.**

It provides the technological foundation on which information systems operate.

A simple way to understand it is:

```text
ORGANIZATION
      ↓
IT INFRASTRUCTURE
      ↓
INFORMATION SYSTEMS
      ↓
INFORMATION
      ↓
BUSINESS ACTIVITIES
      ↓
BUSINESS RESULTS
```

# 3. IT Infrastructure vs Information System

Students often confuse these two concepts.

They are related, but they are not exactly the same.

### IT Infrastructure

Provides the **technological foundation**.

Examples:

- Computers
- Servers
- Networks
- Storage
- Operating systems
- Internet connectivity

### Information System

Uses technology, people, data, and processes to perform a particular organizational purpose.

Examples:

- University ERP
- Banking system
- Payroll system
- Inventory system
- E-commerce system

### Easy Comparison

```text
IT INFRASTRUCTURE
        ↓
Provides the foundation
        ↓
INFORMATION SYSTEM
        ↓
Performs business activities
```

# 22. 🖥️ Mainframe Era

The evolution of IT infrastructure begins with the **mainframe era**.

During this period, organizations depended on large, powerful computers called **mainframes**.

These computers were expensive and usually available only to large organizations such as:

- Banks
- Government organizations
- Large corporations
- Universities
- Research institutions

---

# 23. 🖥️ What Was a Mainframe Computer?

A **mainframe computer** was a large and powerful computer designed to process huge amounts of information and support many users at the same time.

During the mainframe era, organizations generally depended on a central computer rather than giving every employee an individual computer.

A simple structure was:

```text
                 MAINFRAME
                     │
        ┌────────────┼────────────┐
        ↓            ↓            ↓
     TERMINAL      TERMINAL      TERMINAL
        ↓            ↓            ↓
      USER 1        USER 2        USER 3
```

The main processing work was performed by the central mainframe.

---

# 24. 🏢 How Did the Mainframe Era Work?

Imagine a large bank.

The bank needs to process thousands or millions of transactions.

Instead of every employee having a separate system for processing all information, a central mainframe could handle major computing tasks.

The basic process was:

```text
USER
  ↓
TERMINAL
  ↓
CENTRAL MAINFRAME
  ↓
DATA PROCESSING
  ↓
RESULT
  ↓
USER
```

The mainframe therefore acted as the **central computing resource** of the organization.

---

# 25. Why Were Mainframes Important?

Mainframes were important because organizations were beginning to process information on a much larger scale.

They could support activities such as:

- Payroll processing
- Banking transactions
- Inventory processing
- Customer records
- Accounting
- Large-scale data processing

### 🎯 Key Idea

> **The mainframe era introduced large-scale centralized computing into organizations.**

# 27. ⚠️ Limitations of the Mainframe Era

Although mainframes were powerful, they also had some limitations.

### 1. High Cost

Mainframe systems required significant investment.

### 2. Centralized Processing

A large amount of processing depended on the central computer.

### 3. Limited Accessibility

Computing resources were not available to individual users in the way they are today.

### 4. Specialized Management

Organizations required trained technical personnel to operate and maintain these systems.

### 5. Large Physical Infrastructure

Mainframe systems required dedicated physical space and supporting infrastructure.

# 35. 🌐 Client/Server Era

The **Client/Server Era** introduced a more connected approach to computing.

Instead of every computer working completely independently, computers could communicate with servers.

A simple structure was:

```text
             SERVER
            /  |  \
           /   |   \
          ↓    ↓    ↓
      CLIENT CLIENT CLIENT
```

The **client** is generally the user's computer or device.

The **server** provides shared data, applications, or other services.

---

# 36. What Is Client/Server Computing?

In simple language:

> **Client/server computing allows user computers to communicate with servers that provide shared data, applications, or services.**

A simple process is:

```text
USER
  ↓
CLIENT COMPUTER
  ↓
NETWORK
  ↓
SERVER
  ↓
DATA / APPLICATION
  ↓
CLIENT COMPUTER
  ↓
USER
```

---

# 37. Why Did Client/Server Computing Develop?

Organizations needed to overcome some of the limitations of independent personal computers.

They wanted:

- Shared information
- Shared applications
- Centralized data management
- Better communication
- Easier resource sharing
- Better coordination

Client/server systems helped connect users with shared organizational resources.

# 111. 🔍 Step 1 – Identify the Problem

The first step in system building is to understand:

> **What problem does the organization actually need to solve?**

An organization should not build a system simply because new technology is available.

It should first identify the actual business problem.

### Example – University

Suppose students frequently complain:

> "We have to visit different offices to get information about attendance, fees, examination forms, and results."

The problem is not simply:

> "We need an app."

The actual problem is:

> **Students do not have convenient and centralized access to important academic and administrative information.**

> **A good system begins with a clearly understood business problem.**

- Faster implementation

# 140. 🎯 Agile vs Traditional Approach

|---|---|

> **Traditional = Plan → Build → Deliver**

# 172. 🧠 Traditional vs Modern System Building

| Aspect | Traditional Approach | Modern Digital Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Often defined early | Can evolve continuously |
| Development | Larger phases | Smaller cycles |
| User involvement | May be limited | Frequent |
| Feedback | May come later | Continuous |
| Changes | May be difficult | Expected |
| Deployment | Often later | Can be incremental |
| Technology | Mainly internally managed | Cloud and digital platforms increasingly used |
| Access | Often office-focused | Web and mobile access |
| Integration | May be limited | Increasingly important |
| Improvement | Periodic | Continuous |

# 173. ⚠️ Important Point

Modern approaches do not mean that traditional methods have become completely useless.

Different projects have different requirements.

For example:

- A highly regulated system may require extensive planning and documentation.
- A simple mobile application may benefit from rapid iterative development.
- A complex enterprise system may combine several approaches.

Therefore:

> **System development should be selected according to the nature of the project rather than following a single approach blindly.**

# 174. 🎯 Exam-Oriented Definitions

### System Building

> The process of creating or acquiring an information system to solve an organizational problem or support business activities.

### Prototyping

> The development of an early model of a system to understand requirements and obtain user feedback.

### Agile Development

> An approach that develops systems in smaller cycles with frequent feedback and continuous improvement.

### Iterative Development

> A development approach in which a system is developed and improved through repeated cycles.

### Outsourcing

> Obtaining certain technology services or system development activities from an external organization.

### System Integration

> Connecting different systems so that relevant information can be exchanged between them.

### Low-Code Development

> An approach that allows applications to be developed with relatively little traditional programming using visual development tools.

### No-Code Development

> An approach that allows users to create certain applications using visual tools with little or no traditional programming.

### Digital Firm

> An organization in which digital technologies are deeply integrated into business activities, processes, communication, and decision-making.
